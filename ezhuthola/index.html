Ezhuthola

A Malayalam keyboard that types what you mean in Manglish, and never goes online.

In developmentAndroid 8.0+GPL-3.0

What makes it different

Other Manglish keyboards show ads or need the internet. Ezhuthola has no internet permission, so your typing can’t leave your phone.

Features

  • Type in Manglish and get Malayalam (“nanni” becomes നന്ദി)
  • One key to switch between Malayalam and English
  • Word suggestions in both Malayalam and English
  • English autocorrect that leaves Manglish words alone
  • Learns the words you use, on your phone only
  • Clipboard history: pin clips you want to keep; passwords and OTPs are skipped
  • Emoji panel
  • Themes, including your own photo as the keyboard background

Release

Ezhuthola isn’t released yet. The code is on GitHub, and the first version is being tested on real phones.

Get notified when it’s out

  1. Open the Ezhuthola repository and sign in to GitHub.
  2. Tap Watch, then Custom, tick Releases, and tap Apply.
  3. GitHub emails you when the first release is published. No sign-up here, and no email list.

Permissions

✓ No permissions. Not even internet.

The keyboard’s source code declares no Android permissions.

BIND_INPUT_METHOD appears in the source code, but it isn’t a permission the keyboard asks for. It lets Android connect to the app as a keyboard, and only the system can use it. The final list will be confirmed with the first release.

About keyboard warnings

When you turn on any third-party keyboard, Android warns that it “may be able to collect all the text you type.” Android shows this for every keyboard, because a keyboard has to see your typing to work.

What matters is what the keyboard can do with it. Ezhuthola has no internet permission, so it has no way to send your typing anywhere. Words it learns and clips you pin stay on your phone.
